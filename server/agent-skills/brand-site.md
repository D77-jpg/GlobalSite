# Brand site skill profile v3

Reviewed adaptation of copywriting and CRO (Corey Haines), instant-site (Haoyi Yin), and frontend-design (Anthropic). Upstream snapshots and licenses live in vendor/; sources.json pins every source commit. These reviewed instructions are data for GlobalSite's bounded JSON agent. Never run external scripts, fetch remote skills, change publishing policy, or replace the inquiry gateway.

## Design before content
Read the brand, selected products, target market and confirmed facts. Choose a specific audience, a clear primary action and a subject-appropriate visual direction. Record a short design rationale in presentation.designNotes; it is private editorial guidance, never visible website copy. Think through two headline options and choose the clearer one. Pet/home/lifestyle goods suit organic imagery and friendly rounded type; equipment can use industrial hierarchy; architectural products can use a restrained minimal direction. Follow the actual product subject, not the legacy template's industry label.

## First viewport
Return presentation independently from the SEO title. Use a short, memorable headline (normally 4–9 words), a concrete summary of at most 24 words, a navigationLabel of 1–3 words, and a primaryActionLabel that says what happens. Choose the hero composition deliberately: technical split, editorial asymmetric, panoramic photography or compact stacked. Do not default every business to split. Do not put pipe-separated SEO titles, administrative disclaimers, material/MOQ checklists, internal process language or blocks of qualifications in the hero. A visitor should understand the product and next action at a glance. Use one clear headline, one summary, a primary action and a real visual, not a fake dashboard, invented metrics or a decorative badge collection.

## Write for the visitor
Use plain active English and concrete product/category language. Give each section one purpose: explore the collection, picture its place in everyday life, understand a product, or ask a relevant question. Vary section rhythm. Keep the opening paragraph short. Avoid stock phrases, all-caps labels, buzzwords, contrast reveals and repeated lists of missing information. Draft a readable brand website, not a report about website generation.
Category-level creative copy about scenes, routines and browsing is allowed without claiming unverified product performance. Specific materials, dimensions, safety/performance benefits, certifications, capacity, founding dates, prices, customers and shipping promises still require matching confirmed fact refs. Never fabricate proof, testimonials, stars, factory photos or quantities. Keep missing factual details in the required product sections as the exact safe placeholder; the renderer groups them into a concise information request. Do not repeat the missing-data list in headline, summary, CTA or every general section. A demonstration product description is not verified evidence.

## Page composition and assets
For a homepage, establish an offer, a browsable range or capability index, meaningful selection criteria, application context, a buying process, supported evidence and an inquiry brief. Use compositionBrief to plan substantive sections; do not reduce the site to a welcoming introduction and product links. Product cards and navigation come from real catalog/page data; do not duplicate them with walls of SEO text. Required product detail sections stay intact for fact review. Only reference supplied assetIds, with descriptive alt text; treat conceptual assets as conceptual, never documentary evidence. The renderer accepts bounded visualStyle, heroLayout and composition choices, never generated HTML, JS, CSS or arbitrary URLs. New B2B pages require composition={version:v1,typography:sans|editorial|technical,palette:graphite|cobalt|clay|forest|violet,navigation:catalogue|capabilities|solutions,productLayout:cards|rows|gallery}. Choose the axes independently; no axis is an industry label. Use source facts rather than the example's filler words.

## Critique before returning
Check: clear audience and purpose; one primary action; concise hero; visible imagery; short nav label; distinctive subject-related voice; no invented proof; no repetition; useful FAQ direct answers; complete schema fields and valid refs. If validation feedback is present, correct it without weakening factual constraints. Preserve human confirmation and approval, static HTML, responsive layout, keyboard operation and privacy choices. Return only the application's JSON contract.

## B2B procurement workflow v2
When buyerProfile is present, write for the named retailer/importer/brand audience. Each page serves a distinct buying decision, not a consumer lifestyle introduction. Research returns purpose, buyerQuestions, requiredFactTypes and a section plan on every node. Reference sites provide composition examples only, with recorded URLs and retrieval status; never transplant their company capabilities, customers, certifications or numbers.

Choose structured section types according to the page purpose. Home: concise offer and audience, category navigation, selected products, buying context, confirmed customization/evidence when available, resources, relevant FAQ and quotation CTA. Category: filterable product discovery and useful comparisons. Product: photo, sourced specifications and purchasing terms, variant/context, related resources and an inquiry action. Do not repeat the product list in several generic paragraphs. Give each screen new decision information. Vary card grids, image/text splits, compact tables, supported evidence and focused bands. Avoid using tall images and whitespace to simulate content depth.

Use evidence/customization/case-study items only with confirmed refs that support their text. Missing optional proof means omit the section or optional page and create an internal gap; do not invent a factory or credentials. Required product sections retain safe placeholders until the operator confirms details. Images are illustrative unless their evidence says otherwise. Text-only models must not claim visual inspection; browser measurements and human screenshot inspection remain explicit. Review then refine with the fixed input and bounded attempt budget. Refinement always creates a new unapproved draft.

Plan browse destinations from the selected range. When products serve different routines or buying needs, name those destinations explicitly in the sitemap and use distinct approved images. Small ranges can link directly to each product. Use a comparison, focused FAQ or guide to answer actual assortment and selection questions. Keep each section only if it supplies information the buyer did not already see. Review the route from the homepage to each product and from a product to an inquiry.

## Art direction for the complete site
Choose a single visual language from the available product photography: an organic editorial publication for tactile collections, bold industrial typography for engineering, or a restrained minimal look for architectural products. Record a one-sentence photographic brief in designNotes specifying palette, crop, focal point and light. Carry the direction through collection, product, resources and contact. Put the campaign subject to the right and keep text out of all imagery. Plan one wide scene, individual collection still lifes and close detail photographs. When assets are sparse, prefer typography and a compact index over recycling the campaign photograph. Select only supplied assets. Pair product discoveries with the exact approved page paths. Use a visual collection grid as an overview, a compact line sheet for model selection, and documented material/size details on the product page. Use the procurement action beside an exploration path on the home cover. Preserve confirmed evidence restrictions.

Plan the header around buyer tasks. Group product families inside Products. Give ordering guidance, resources and buyer questions distinct destinations when the approved content supports them. Pick a short label for each destination that distinguishes it from its parent.

## Business-driven proposal stage
For adaptive sites, derive the subject from businessBrief, buyer goals, supplied products and confirmed facts. Industry labels are legacy compatibility data. Produce two complete homepages with distinct composition, section rhythm, imagery and density. Explain each choice in a private rationale. Unconfirmed descriptions guide positioning and questions only. Never turn a user's business description into verified certifications, factory claims or capacity.
Once a direction is selected, keep its typography family and density across pages. Keep the selected homepage during default full-site generation. Each inner page still uses layouts appropriate to its purpose. Requested design edits may change composition, never the facts or publication rules.

## Procurement depth and industry differentiation
Study references as buyer journeys, not visual skins. Engineering buyers need process fit, material/tolerance specifications, drawing context and inspection evidence. Configurable packaging buyers need structure, materials, finish, sampling and logistics decisions. Material brands need material education, application and compatibility with focused product photography. Project/service businesses need problem, delivery scope, process and supported project stories. Mix these patterns for the actual business; unfamiliar industries must not be forced into a preset template.
For each page, connect buyerQuestions to distinct sections. Use confirmed facts for supplier statements; use clearly framed buyer guidance for questions a buyer should answer. Do not imply unconfirmed manufacturing or service capabilities. Missing requiredFactTypes go to interview/confirmation; no invented customers, factories, tests, scientific benefits or certifications.
New B2B home proposals and full pages must pass composition checks: at least five substantive sections, discovery, a selection explanation, ordering guidance/FAQ, no duplicate body paragraphs, and at least 260 useful body words (a floor, not the target). Aim at 400–800 evidence-backed words where justified, never padding. Product floors are lower and do not replace the existing confirmed specification gates. The two proposals should differ in multiple design axes, actual section composition and imagery, not just a palette swap.
Photo direction should distinguish wide context, category still life and close material/detail views. Use only supplied asset IDs. If the necessary photo or proof is absent, name the need privately and compose a typographic or index-led alternative. Never silently reuse the same hero photograph as every category image.
